Why these six newsrooms?

This is the biggest judgment call on the page, and no AI made it.

Panel size decides what agreement looks like.
Panel size decides what agreement looks like. Six outlets make unanimity easy; thirty would make it rare. Every count on this page is relative to these six and to nothing wider. This is the most consequential judgment NewsLens makes and it is not made by the AI.
How this panel was picked. The panel is chosen per story, not fixed across the site. For this story the panel is outlets that ran original coverage of the BLS release within 24 hours, spanning a business desk, a markets desk, general broadcast news, public radio, and a policy think tank.
Why it changes per story. A jobs report is covered by business and economics desks. A sports story or a health story would draw a completely different panel, and the counts on this page would mean nothing if applied to one.
Who chose it. The panel was chosen by the designer, Milan Khanal. No outlet was excluded for what it said. Reuters, the Associated Press, and The New York Times were attempted and blocked automated retrieval, so they were left out rather than quoted secondhand.
Not built yet. Readers cannot currently add or remove an outlet and watch the counts change. That is the single most important missing feature, because it is the one that would hand this judgment to the reader.

How was this comparison made?

The machinery, in plain words. Open it whenever you want; it never opens itself.

ⓘ Five steps, one model, one human. See inside open the glass box
1We captured each outlet's article on Jul 2–3, 2026, and dated the snapshots (outlets edit stories after publishing).
2An AI read each article and listed the factual claims it makes. What counts as a "claim" is defined in Methods; that boundary is a choice, so we disclose it.
3Claims saying the same thing were grouped, so we can count who reported what. Counting is the only math on this site.
4A human reviewed every extraction against a published checklist. Fidelity fixes only, never adding, removing, or rewording claims by preference. All 10 claims in this story were reviewed against the six live articles on July 20, 2026. Every sentence shown on this page was copied from the article, not summarised. Three of the six articles were edited after publication, so each sentence carries a capture date. Corrections made during the very first design-phase pass were not logged as they happened, and that gap is disclosed rather than backfilled.
5Each claim shows a confidence level, and anything the AI might have gotten wrong stays flaggable by you.
Model cardExtraction: Claude (Anthropic) - exact model version being confirmed for the model card. Built by a third party, trained on web-scale data that itself contains media bias. Known failure modes and the extraction prompt: in Methods.
What this pipeline tends to get wrongMerging claims that say almost the same thing, missing claims phrased as implication rather than statement, and over-splitting compound sentences. Every extraction was human-reviewed; this story's corpus is small, so we publish patterns, not percentages.
What was filtered outSome sentences failed extraction or matching and never reached this page. They were not recorded for this story's first pass; they are recorded for every story after it.
SyndicationSyndication overlap between outlets has not yet been annotated for this story.
What we never doNo bias labels. No truth verdicts. No engagement optimization. The prototype itself was designed and built with AI assistance (Claude), human-directed. Disclosed because we hold ourselves to the same standard.
